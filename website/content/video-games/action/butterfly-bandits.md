It's just a game about **catching butterflies.**

It's 3D. Butterflies (or other insects) fly around through the environment. (Could be fixed levels, could be randomly generated with some simple water + vegetation.)

All players have this big net to catch them with.

**Your only job is to catch the right ones** (and not catch the wrong ones).

-   This could be **cooperative:** reach a certain goal, or fulfill a "recipe" of things to catch
-   This could be **competitive:** score the most points within the time limit.

But it should be a *really* simple game. Basic (low-poly?) 3D environments. Characters that walk and jump, with a net sticking out at a certain side.

> The net feels most useful if it sticks out *horizontally*, as then you can catch things nicely by rotating around.
>
> Some insect powers might *displace* your net, though?
>
> Players might also be butterfly-like-creatures themselves. Or maybe that's too much.

Notes:

-   Different insects fly in different patterns. Some are more *floaty swirly* like Butterflies. Others are chaotic, like *wasps*. Some are fast, some are slow. Some land on branches, some don't. (Are there are also non-flying insects to catch? Seems to break the pattern too much.)
-   Different insects have different rules or powers. When caught, they might activate in some way. Maybe certain insects can *not* be combined within your net: you need to empty/clean/release first.
    -   Example: an insect that releases a gas/poison/smoke bomb
    -   Example: an insect that, when caught, allows you to *fly* for a bit.
    -   *In general, catching an insect changes how you play from that moment until the next thing you catch*.
