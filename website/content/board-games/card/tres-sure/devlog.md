# Devlog

(Keep it short, just to gather my thoughts.)

So, the game "Exploding Kittens" has been a well-known (and well-liked) staple of many game nights across many groups. It's very accessible, often leads to very funny situations, and is just a solid little card game anyone could play.

However, that doesn't mean it's perfect. In fact, it's far from perfect when you play the game more often and start "expecting" it to be more than it is. (By adding, for example, expansions.)

As usual, this makes me wonder: could I make my own little card game that copies the *good* bits and improves on the *bad* bits?

Having some time to spare here and there, I started working on a plan.

## What are the issues?

Here are my issues with "Exploding Kittens":

**Issue #1:** Once you're dead ... well, you're dead, the game is really boring, and you might be doing nothing for a whole lotta time.

**Issue #2:** While there is *some* strategy and *some* certainty, it's still a very random game. Even cards that might give you a strategy, often end up just doing random stuff.

(Example: you're often stuck with a bunch of "*See the Future"* cards that allow you to check what's on top of the deck ... but without any card that can actually *change* that. So it's useless and whether you lose or not is still random.)

**Issue #3:** The decision to move all text onto the *cards* (to prevent people from having to read rules or memorize icons) is an admirable one, but doesn't work in practice.

It just means people have to read a tiny paragraph of text *every time they draw a new card*. (Only experienced players will be able to tell immediately by color/title/artwork. But by the time you're experienced ... you could also have just read a short list of rules.)

**Issue #4:** The parts that *do* have strategy often end up being a case of "politics". People are like "hey, I was nice to you once, don't screw me over now, right?" And often enough, people actually listen, and they might band together against someone else who "always wins games" or something.

This means that the game isn't about playing the best/most fun game anymore, but just about managing a complex set of relationships within any group. (Which, if you want to do that and enjoy that, you achieve more easily with anything *but* a silly card game.)

**Issue #5:** Either decisions are obvious, or you don't make a decision at all. *Many turns* in Exploding Kittens are just "well, can't do anything, let's just draw a card and hope for the best". At other times, you're sure a kitten is at the top of the deck, and you have one card that shuffles the deck. So, well, the decision is kinda obvious eh?

There's no real choice here. No doubt about what to do. No reason to always be active and *make stuff happen*.

(Most people hold onto their "nope" card until the very last moment, when suddenly *all of the nopes come out*. The "attack" card is way stronger than most other cards in the game, so you would never play it just for the sake of it. These are almost certainties in any group I've played, experienced or not.)

**Issue #6:** There's no sense of *progress* in the game. You might be playing for twenty minutes, and nobody actually achieved anything, or might have collected significantly better cards than at the start, or *any kind of progress*. It can all change in a flash. And that's fine ... if the game was *much shorter* and *leaned even more into the randomness/chaotic nature of it*.

## What are the good bits?

**Good Bit #1:** the game is *all about cards*. There are no other tokens, or pawns, or whatever. This greatly simplifies the game, declutters the table, makes it easy and quick to setup (or put back into the box).

(Which is why I was annoyed that one of the expansions has a "cone of shame". Yeah, hilarious when you think about it as a designer, but in practice just a very stupid thing to add to a game that is otherwise *only same-sized cards*. My playing groups basically threw it away after considering for five seconds.)

**Good Bit #2:** the game is all about the *most basic of turns (in a typical card game*): play cards, draw new ones. Most people already know this. It doesn't get much simpler than this.

Having just a *single stack of cards*, of which the drawing order is very important, feeds into that system well.

**Good Bit #3:** the threat of elimination is always a good one for adding tension to a game. (I once wrote a whole article on it, on my Dutch blog, but the idea is simply: elimination is a great tool for games, as long as you find strong ways to mitigate the issue of players doing nothing for 50% of the game.)

**Good Bit #4:** the base game actually doesn't have that many cards. Only once you see some of the expansions, do you realize there are *loads of basic/intuitive/obvious actions* they did not include in the base game.

(Example: the *Reverse* card, which reverses order of play, only appears in a later expansion.)

This is good, because I've learned over the years that designers (including myself) have a tendency to try and cram *all ideas they have* into the game. Which just makes it overwhelming, confusing, like a math exercise instead of a fun game.

To be honest, when I really looked into the rules (and played the game more often with different people), I realized that it's really *not as simple as everyone (and their website) states*.

(And that we might be moving in a backward direction when it comes to attention spans, but that's another topic.)

There's the fact that you can either *pass* or *play* on your turn, which confuses people. The fact that there are these cat cards you can collect *besides* the action cards, which confuses people, which do different things when played in different numbers, *which confuses people*.

It's really not *that* simple or accessible. I think the core idea ("explode = dead, don't explode = win") is what keeps it all together and helps people get into it, as the game has more rules and kinks than many other "simple" games.

## What do we learn from this?

I want a game where:

-   You can be eliminated. But it's a softer elimination and there's more gray area to keep "dead" players active.

-   It's all about cards. No matter what I want to do or accomplish, *holding/playing cards is the only thing in the game*.

-   There's one big draw pile in the center of the table. Its contents, its order, what you draw is of the utmost importance.

-   It feels a bit more adventurous, special and strategic than Exploding Kittens.

Quickly I realized the concept of a *treasure hunt* would fit really well.

The stack in the center of the table is the "digging pile". It represents a "cave" or a "mission".

Each turn, you dig further into the ground, *drawing cards from the deck*, uncovering treasures or dangers.

Everyone plays with the goal of gaining the most treasure ... and staying alive to keep it.

That was the basic idea. Where can we go from here?

### Objective?

How do you gain treasure? As it's all about cards: **there is treasure on the cards!**

Each card simply shows two things:
-   Its own action/function
-   A treasure value (in the corner, perhaps)

Cards are identified by **color, (short) title and pattern/image.** If you want to know what something does, check a single page in the rulebook, I'm not putting *tiny paragraphs of text* on all cards.

Why do I think this is a good idea? Because it gives *real choice*. It makes turns *not obvious*. Do I play this card now for its action? Or do I want to keep it because it's such high value? But treasure is worthless if I'm dead ... so what to do?

The game will be a balance: try to just barely stay alive, while keeping as much treasure as possible.

### Gameplay?

In Exploding Kittens, you can play as many cards as you want ... including 0. Then you must draw 1.

This is what leads to turns of inactivity, as there's no reason to play a card (and you're not required to), and people might be really low on options (as you only draw back 1).

So I invented a rule I think is better: **play as many cards as you want, but at least 1 (if possible). At the end of your turn, *draw* as many cards as you played.**

You must do *something*, so no inactive/void turns. At the same time, it gives another *real choice*: how many cards am I willing to play? With each card played, I need to draw one more, increasing the risk of drawing something *bad*, but also the chance of drawing something *good*.

### Immediate Issues

I see two small issues here.

**Issue #1:** If it's always "draw as many as you played", hand size stays constant the whole game. Not fun, too strict and predictable.

Solution? Add cards to the base game that *modify* this rule. (Allowing you to draw *more* cards than played, or *fewer*, or not play at all but draw all the same.)

**Issue #2:** If all your treasure is in your hand, the winning condition is perhaps *too* brittle. It's too easy to be forced to play away all your treasure, or have it stolen from you in literally the last round.

Solution? You can *store* treasure. This simply means placing the card facedown in front of you. Now it is safe and nobody can touch it, not even you. (Barring some special action cards that may do something with stored treasure.)

I'm not sure if storing should be a "free action", or require using another "special card", or have other restrictions. But in general this seems a strong and sensible idea.

Over time, people will get higher and higher stored piles in front of them, giving some sense of who is winning, or general *progress*. (Another issue solved!)

## Soft(er) Elimination

How do we ensure dead players aren't just going to walk away or sit there frustrated for twenty minutes?

**First Modification:** players have multiple lives. This is also true in *Exploding Kittens* (you start with one Defuse card for sure, but can get more over time) ... just less explicit. Which confused some people.

So let's make it explicit. You start with X "life" cards in your hand. Over time, these will be removed by all sorts of threats. If you have no lives in your hand, you're dead.

(This is more forgiving, as you have more leeway. It's rare to be completely alive one turn and dead the next. You'll lose lives over time, due to your own bad decisions :p)

**Second Modification:** dead players become *ghosts*.

I envisioned multiple cool things for ghosts to do, which also made me split my ideas into a "campaign", so you can *choose* which variant of the game you want to play.

(Always recommended. Usually, a game can be broken down into smaller parts, and teaching a smaller part to new players is *always* preferable.)

**Ghosts #1:** they get one more *reverse turn*. Place as many cards as they want *on top of the digging stack*, then draw equally many cards from the *playing stack*.

(They can use this turn at any point until the end of the game. They don't get their regular turns anymore.)

**When you die, you choose someone new to support.** Place your indicator near that person. If they win, you win. (Probably need to include 10 cards with different colors, so players can choose a color and use that as their "indicator".)

**Ghosts #2:** adds cards with a *ghost action* on it. Ghosts still get turns, but may only play ghost actions.

(These are different from the card's normal action, and usually a bit weaker.)

This means you can *prepare* for dying. You still have quite a lot to do. At the same time, you're not as strong as a living player. And you *will* run out of ghost action cards and not be able to do many turns anymore.

**Ghosts #3:** adds cards with a *curse value* on it. (Next to the treasure value.) As a ghost, your final score are your *curse points*. So you can still win if that outranks the player with the highest *treasure points*.

(I don't want to add this to the base card set. It would just confuse new players and make the explanation harder. So, to compensate, those curse values will need to be much *higher* than treasure values on average, as they'll appear less often. If I balance this right, winning as a ghost is more risky and takes more skill, but is doable.)

## A Base Game

Exploding Kittens has *8* action cards in the base game + *5* types of cats you can collect.

Let's try to not go *above* that.

We need cards that:

-   Change the digging stack order

-   Change how much you can or must play/draw

-   Can somehow attack other players (steal their treasure, or insert traps into the stack)

-   Actually take away player's lives

Changing the stack order:

-   "Shuffle": check the top X cards and put them back in any order

-   "Insert": insert one of your cards into the digging stack, you *may* draw two more as a reward.

Changing your playing/drawing:

-   "You *don't* need to draw a card for all cards played before this one"

-   "Skip drawing cards (at the end of this turn)"

Attack:

-   "Steal": ask someone for a specific treasure value; if they have a card like that, they must give it to you

-   "Skip a turn (place in front of someone, can be yourself)"

Lives:

-   "Spikes": immediately discard one life. Put this card back into the stack wherever you like.

-   "Trap": negates one life, but don't need to discard or reveal it. (Unless you're completely dead.)

### Issues

Again, I see some issues:

**Issue #1:** Cards like the "trap" would only be revealed if someone dies, and thus never re-enter the stack.

Solution? I should add a rule like "when you die, put all life cards back into the stack". Makes sense anyway: over time, the number of dangers ramps up.

**Issue #2:** What if someone is out of cards? They can never get any now, because you draw as many as you play :p

Solution? A rule like "if you can't play a card on your turn, you draw two from the *bottom* of the stack"

**Issue #3:** How do we store treasure now? Or do I leave that for the next step in the campaign?

The more I think about it, the more it seems like a vital feature. It should be in the base game, and it should be so simple that it's not a barrier.

Here's a simple rule: you may store *one card* each turn. For free.

I thought this would be too generous and advantageous. But ... it does mean you're down a card. And you can't be sure if you needed the action on it. So it's still not really an obvious decision, especially if you can only do one per turn.

### Sets?

However, fully realizing this base game *did* show me why the creators of Exploding Kittens decided to add that little "set collection" mechanic to their game.

It just solves a lot of issues in an interesting way. (What if all your cards are horrible? Use them as a set for something else! How to prevent someone getting an extremely powerful hand of cards? Don't make *all cards* have an action.)

I **don't** want to add extra types just as collectibles. (Nor do I like the idea of "Have 2? They do this. Have 3? They do this *different thing*!", but I have to admit it's great for gameplay.)

Instead, let's make sets based on **treasure value**. (A number in the range of 0-10 that *every* card should have.) Let's find a very simple, but helpful rule.

You can play *sets*: multiple cards at the same time. In that case, their action is *not* executed, they have a special set action that replaces it.

Just some ideas ...

**Play 2 cards with the *same* treasure to look at equally many cards at the top of the deck.** (Play two "5s" to see 5 cards.)

**Play 3 cards with the *same* treasure to steal equally many cards from other players.** (Yes, that's powerful ... but losing 3 cards with high treasure is also quite a cost to pay.)

**If you play a combination of cards that sums to 10+ treasure, look at the top 10 cards of the deck and put them back however you want.**

I always try to keep the rules for the "base game" one page. (Plus one "appendix" page with the individual card types and what they do.) Adding sets makes this impossible, so they *will* have to move to the next level.

## Testing

At this point, I felt like I just had to do a quick mockup of these cards and rules, and test it.

(I can think and reason all I want, seeing the game in action will usually reveal all the flaws, and also all the things that are better than I expected.)

Even though I'm completely unsure about all these rules and if the game will be balanced/fun/playable at all, this is the only way forward.
