# \[Devlog\] Conductors of the Underground

Welcome to another boardgame devlog!

In this one, I will explain how I made my game **Conductors of the Underground \<TO DO: Link>**.

However, this one will be a bit different than my other devlogs, because the whole development for this game took some surprising turns.

If you haven't played the game yet or looked at its web page, I strongly suggest doing so, otherwise this whole article won't make much sense. Just in case, here's a very short summary of the game idea and theme:

Hades has some trouble controlling his underworld. There are just too many souls arriving! Help him send each soul to their final destination, as quickly as possible, to win the prestigious title of "Conductor of the Underground". Draw metro lines, collect spirits, send them on their way, and make good use of all the special stations on the board.

## What's different about this game?

Usually, I start with a strong unique idea, which usually comes to me unexpectedly when I stand in the shower or cycle on my bike. (For example, "wouldn't it be cool if you could *cut* into your game board" was the starting point for Unstable Universe.)

Once I have that first spark of imagination, I develop the idea into a first draft of the rules. And when I'm fairly certain how it will work, I start creating the website and game boards.

This time, it was the other way around.

Quite some time ago, I stumbled upon the games **Let's Make a Bus Route** and **Metro X**. Both use transit maps (with roads and subway lines, respectively) on which you must *draw* your optimal route, while cards that are randomly drawn influence your decisions.

I really liked these games, for two reasons:

-   They are often really easy to setup, teach and play. (You don't need to explain people where they can or can't move, for example, because the board already indicates the roads. And everyone likes to draw stuff.)

-   I like making beautiful (randomly generated) game boards, and subway maps are a prima candidate for that.

The first reason made me start development of this game. The second reason made me start with the **game board**, instead of the rules.
