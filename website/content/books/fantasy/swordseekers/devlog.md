# Swordseekers Diary

Welcome to the "writer's diary" for Swordseekers (@TODO: Link). As I work on projects, I always write down my thought process and other interesting choices made along the way. This might be fun or educational for others, but it also helps me think through problems and ideas.

Obviously, there will be **spoilers** for the whole story! Even for things that did _not_ make it into the final story ;) 

So let's get started! 

## What's the idea?

I realized that many stories had great concepts, but were hard to grasp because their ideas were a bit ... abstract. I wanted to find a way to make magic entirely _physical_ (and thus _visual_). A way that would be easy to imagine in your head, or to produce on film, or to imitate for kids as they play in real life.

> Harry Potter, for example, was always a bit of a pain to imitate in real life. I remember trying to play stories in that world with friends or family, and it was always a letdown. Because how does magic work? You say some made-up word, then wave a wooden stick. The same very minimal action, and you could invent literally _any_ consequence for it. If you take away the wand, with nonverbal spells from the later books, it becomes even more vague and abstract.

Rather quickly, I realized Swords were a good answer. I wrote down an idea for a world in which _all_ magic (**without** exceptions) came from ...

* Holding a unique sword that did one unique thing
* And swinging or using it in the right way

Merely holding the sword is not enough: you have to _do_ something with it. 

Swords are not wands: each sword has only one unique type of magic, which can't be found in _any_ other sword.

At the time, I wrote the idea in Dutch. I'd only written Dutch stories so far and hadn't decided to switch to English yet. The original title was a pun on the Dutch words for sword and gravity, which, as you can see, does not translate to English. In the process of converting my notes to English, I found the new working title "Swordseekers".

The main characters were called Nimbe and Harpe from the start. No idea why.

I decided to make it a middle-grade book. Especially boys around that age go through a "sword phase". At times, I wondered if it shouldn't be (young) adult literature, especially because there will probably be blood and death a world that revolves around swords. I decided against it, simply because I had to make a decision, and all my notes leaned towards middle-grade.

### Also, images

I wanted to start every chapter with an image of a new sword, including some description or properties of it. Such images really make a story come alive, in my eyes, and are a way to provide extra information all the time (that isn't boring or more text).

Also, I like challenging myself with experimental structures, and wanted to get more drawing practice.

## Turning tiny ideas into story

How do we extract a story from this? Where do we start? What do our main characters want?

I put the idea on the backburner for a time, as I finished other projects. During that period, a new idea would regularly pop into my head (for a new type of sword, for example), and I'd write it down.

By the time I could start writing this story, I had about 10 pages of "ideas" (about plot twists, scenes, the world, the magic) that all seemed promising and nicely connected ... but still no _story_.

### Starting with Plot Twists

I started with the plot twists. These came to me randomly and I could've used them in other stories, but I found this idea to be the best fit. To get a plot, I merely had to invent situations that would _lead_ to the plot twists.

* **Twist 1**: from the start, Nimbe and Harpe are portrayed as living in the same country and considering the other country evil and bad. Then it's revealed that they're actually _not_ from the same country.
* **Twist 2**: Nimbe and Harpe both find a new Sword, which they both regularly visit to figure out how to wield it. I do my best to make you believe they are different swords, but they're actually the same.
* **Twist 3**: While researching the legend of Arthur (which hugely inspired this idea, of course), I noticed that history books barely even mention him. Then I found out about an influential historic work of which half the stories were just ... made up. Arthur was probably just a "pretty good fighter" that was embellished in storytelling. 
  * I thought this was interesting: a storyline in which you research history, but you can't find the origin of something everyone believes, and later realize half of what you've been taught is just made-up.
  * At this time, I didn't know yet how to incorporate it.
* **Twist 4**: The whole book, people are looking for the Swordsmith. Near the climax, it's revealed that one of the friends Nimbe makes has been the Swordsmith all along.

The fourth twist, and precisely how it plays out, was actually a dream I had. I just repurposed it to belong to this story.

This isn't my usual workflow, by the way. I try to experiment with new story structures every project. This time, my brain thought: "let's start with _plot twists_!"

### An inciting incident

From earlier stories, I learned not to make the inciting incident ( = the event that kicks off the whole story) too small or insignificant. Ideally, you want the first chapter to be a promise as to what the _main story_ is going to be about. There can be many subplots, other ideas, side characters, etcetera---but if you start with those, the reader will expect _those_ to be the meat of the story. And they'll be confused or disappointed if that's untrue.

As such, I came up with a simple conflict that would affect both main characters, and which could sustain the whole story:

> The Eversword was found!

In the early version, this sword was called the "Sword of Might" or "Sword of Power". It sounded a bit cliché to me (and a bit long to keep repeating in the first chapter), so I changed it.

This sword is rumored to be extremely powerful. If you have it, world dominance is basically guaranteed. As such, the two countries of this world immediately prepare search parties and prepare for war.

This is the _overarching_ story. The main thread. It keeps the story together from start to finish, no matter what happens.

* Two countries are both preparing for war
* As they are both searching for a way to get that Eversword.

There are two steps to getting a sword: finding it and knowing how to _get_ it. When the rumor is spread, it has been found, but the one who discovered it could not figure out how to possess it. So, everyone knows it exists, but nobody actually has it yet.

### Filling in the gaps

Okay. Great. Now let's see if we can fill in the gaps. 

The issue with plot twists, is that they must be obvious in hindsight, but not before that moment. It would be very easy to slip up and clearly communicate that Nimbe and Harpe are from two different countries, for example.

Or, if I let them both find a mysterious sword at the start ... most readers will already guess the plot twist of it being the same sword.

As such, I mostly had to come up with smart ways to "obfuscate" this information. This step actually solved most issues and gave me my story.

* Nimbe has to _flee_ in her first chapter. This brings her close to the border.
* There she visits a "magic school" where she learns Swordseeking. With this, she can make friends (and meet the Swordsmith), learn more about that history of sword magic.
* She finds the mysterious sword _very quickly_. It's written from the perspective of "yeah, school is teaching me well, but I also need to _hide_ this from my teachers"
* Harpe does the opposite. His family actually controls part of the border. They stay and let the enemy in. (Now we have Nimbe and Harpe very close to each other, while still being in different countries!)
* He is _rejected_ from the magic school, and thus has to go on a more "rogue" adventure to seek swords. He meets up with enemy search parties, visits enemy villages, which allows me to "convince" the reader that he's from the same country as Nimbe for a while.
* He finds the mysterious sword _much later_. It's written from the perspective of "I need this sword to take revenge, no matter what", and I hope the different context is enough to hide the plot twist.

In general, I am very interested in showing problems from all different angles. That's why I have two main characters I wanted to respond _differently_ to the same call.

The Eversword is found?

* Nimbe's family _flees_. They do not want war. They don't want to be _drafted_ into the army.
* Harpe's family _does nothing_. They think the current state of the country is dreadful and would welcome new leaders.
* (Along the way, they meet others whose family choose the third option: send everyone into war and fight.)

The rest of the story is basically about the consequences of these interesting, crucial actions from the first chapters.

The rejection of Harpe is both an unexpected event and crucial for his character arc. (He starts out with a lot of injustice, which leads to anger and revenge. Over time, he learns to control this and becomes more soft.)

Nimbe has the opposite arc. She starts out being protected and fleeing from any conflict. As she gets more powerful and finds swords, however, she becomes aggressive and seeks to fight injustice violently.

As expected, these two main characters will meet up regularly and influence each other's arc.

## Promises

Now came the time to write those first chapters. One for Nimbe, one for Harpe.

What's important here?

* An interesting hook for the opening paragraphs.
* The inciting incident happens at the end. Clearly define the hero's goal from now on and why this shakes up their life.
* Between those, in the meat of the chapter, promise the reader what the story is about and what they can expect.

To me, these were the things I had to promise.

* There'll be lots of (sword)fighting, and magic comes from wielding those swords.
* The main thread is a war between two countries with opposing views on the magic. 
* The specifics of their storylines. (Nimbe will reach a magic school soon, Harpe will come into contact with secret enemy Swordsweekers.)

As such, I wanted to add action in the first chapter. I could've made Nimbe's family flee peacefully. I could've turned it into a spy chapter, in which they merely _hide_ from soldiers looking for them. But that would've promised the wrong thing, as it's not a spy novel.

No, I gave her father a Sword, and he has to engage in a short battle to let his family flee.

Similarly, this is why I changed Harpe's family to be "border control". This way, I could easily sneak-explain information about the two countries and how different they are. (Without giving away the plot twist.)

As for the specifics, 

* Nimbe could simply be comforted by their parents with talks of Swordseekers. Or it might be a rumor she hears in their new town (once they arrive).
* Harpe's first chapter is literally about letting enemy Swordseekers through their gates.

To further communicate Harpe's _aggressiveness_, and sword magic in general, I wanted to give him a sword. But if he already had a sword, he felt too powerful. Why wouldn't he enact revenge _now_? What would be enough motivation for him to still search the Eversword? It felt convoluted.

As such, I gave him a sword, but he has no clue how it works. It's soft. It disappears if you try to hit someone else. It's useless to him, but he carries it around _bluffing_ that he's a powerful Swordbearer.

I thought those chapters would be interesting, action-packed, and make all the right promises. With that certainty, I finally started writing.
